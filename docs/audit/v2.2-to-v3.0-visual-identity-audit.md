# v2.2 → v3.0 Visual Identity Architecture Audit

Date: 2026-08-20

## Trigger

A production exercise exposed a systemic failure: v2.2 could perform rigorous strategy, research, direction selection, UI and QA while still allowing an arbitrary color/type foundation to become official simply because Art Direction had described it and Design System had converted it into tokens.

The failure was not “bad color taste”. It was missing ownership and evidence between **direction** and **systemization**.

## Audit question

Does the OS model the work required to create a high-end professional digital design system, where typography, color and graphic language are derived from project evidence, explored as alternatives, pressure-tested and independently validated before UI production?

## Benchmark observations

The benchmark is not a price claim. The goal is to replicate observable disciplines used by established brand/digital studios in complex professional engagements.

### Work & Co

Source: https://www.work.co/process/

Observed principles:

- small senior multidisciplinary teams;
- technology involved from the beginning;
- prototypes early rather than late-stage reveals;
- ongoing testing and validation;
- continuous refinement instead of a one-shot design handoff.

Implication for OS: visual identity cannot be a one-agent aesthetic choice; feasibility and validation must happen before production lock.

### Instrument

Sources:

- https://www.instrument.com/work
- publicly documented identity/digital case studies reviewed during audit (including Eames Institute, Marriott, Oura, Sonos, ServiceNow and Shutterfly).

Observed principles:

- strategy/brand perception feeds visual language;
- typography, color, photography, iconography and motion are treated as an integrated system;
- prototypes/native digital applications are used to judge behavior;
- type/color decisions are connected to brand/product context rather than generic web styling.

Implication for OS: Strategy needs a perception contract; Research needs brand/category foundations; identity needs application tests.

### COLLINS

Source: https://www.wearecollins.com/

Case-study patterns reviewed during audit included Mailchimp, Bose, Match and Arcane.

Observed principles:

- visual systems are rooted in a strategic/cultural/product territory;
- typography may be custom or highly specific when the concept warrants it;
- color and type are pressure-tested across different touchpoints and scales;
- distinctiveness is systemic, not a single decorative effect.

Implication for OS: a candidate must survive an exchange/pressure test and cannot be approved because it looks “premium”.

### Pentagram

Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work

Case-study patterns reviewed during audit included PayPal, OPPO, Pfizer and Isomorphic Labs.

Observed principles:

- typography/color are first-class identity components;
- custom type is used when strategically justified, not as a generic quality signal;
- color systems can deliberately retain, expand or reduce category equities;
- identity systems cover multiple media/communication modes.

Implication for OS: type/color require an owner distinct from UI tokenization and must support `inherit | evolve | establish` contexts.

## v2.2 structural findings

### F1 — No Visual Identity owner

04 owned “principios tipográficos/cromáticos”. 05 converted direction into semantic tokens. No discipline owned the evidence, sourcing and synthesis of a real typography/color foundation.

Severity: CRITICAL.

### F2 — G2 Research could be too broad / inspiration-led

Research produced visual territory but did not require a brand audit, typography landscape, color/material/product landscape or explicit adjacent/cultural references.

Severity: HIGH.

### F3 — DRS could skip foundational research

`NO_EXTERNAL_RESOURCE_REQUIRED` was a useful default for routine components, but the same policy could be applied when choosing typography/color. This allowed foundational choices to inherit intuition without targeted sourcing.

Severity: CRITICAL.

### F4 — No identity candidate competition

G5 required multiple experience directions, but after V00 there was no separate competition between identity foundations within the selected direction.

Severity: HIGH.

### F5 — No controlled pressure test

Type/color could be judged inside different layouts/creative directions. That contaminates comparison: it is impossible to know whether the preference came from identity or composition.

Severity: HIGH.

### F6 — No independent identity validator

V00 validates experience direction. V01 validates tasks. Neither is responsible for whether typography/color/graphic language actually belong to the project.

Severity: CRITICAL.

### F7 — Accessibility/performance arrived after identity had effectively been locked

12/09 advised Design System and Motion, but webfont licensing/charset/loading and color/media compatibility were not explicit pre-system identity concerns.

Severity: HIGH.

### F8 — Existing visual decisions could become implicit equities

A project Design DNA could preserve historical palette/type hypotheses without a status that distinguishes `human preference`, `existing equity`, `research hypothesis` and `G6-approved foundation`.

Severity: HIGH.

## v3 architecture

```text
G0  Context + identity mode
G1  Strategy + Perception
G2  Brand / Category / Visual Foundations Research
G3  UX
G4  Content
G5  Experience Direction + V00
G6  Visual Identity + IV01
G7  Design System
G8  UI
G9  Responsive
G10 Motion
G11 Red Team
G12 Frontend
G13 Visual QA
G14 A11y / Performance
G15 Release
```

## G6 contract

Owner: `VI01`

Required evidence:

- identity sourcing research;
- typography system;
- color system;
- visual identity system;
- identity pressure tests;
- DRS identity review;
- accessibility advisory;
- frontend feasibility;
- media compatibility;
- independent IV01 validation.

## Key safeguards

1. `visual_identity_mode` must resolve to `inherit | evolve | establish` before G0 approval.
2. G2 external visual-foundations research is mandatory for every profile.
3. Art Direction only defines provisional type/color territories.
4. Foundational DRS research cannot terminate in `NO_EXTERNAL_RESOURCE_REQUIRED`.
5. Typography candidates require real source/foundry, web license, charset and production characteristics.
6. Color candidates require project-specific provenance/context, not generic color psychology.
7. Profiles require 2/3/4 identity candidates.
8. Candidates share base layout/copy/media for identity pressure tests.
9. IV01 performs exchange test and `KEEP | ITERATE | KILL` verdicts.
10. 05 cannot repair/change foundations silently.
11. UI/Responsive/Motion/Frontend/QA reopen G6 when real application disproves a foundation.
12. CI mutation tests protect identity-validation and candidate minimum semantics.

## What v3 deliberately does NOT do

- It does not promise that a process automatically produces a €40k-quality result.
- It does not equate expensive/custom fonts with quality.
- It does not require a particular agency tool, Figma workflow or paid provider.
- It does not use scoring as an automatic aesthetic selector.
- It does not universalize color psychology.
- It does not require two fonts or a fixed palette size.
- It does not make every small project perform the same volume of exploration; profiles scale depth but preserve core ownership/evidence.

## Expected result

The system should now fail earlier and more usefully. A weak palette or typography choice should be rejected during G6 because its origin, behavior, media fit, accessibility, production viability or specificity cannot be defended — not discovered after an expensive UI build.
