# Selecting effects

Effects are design candidates, not mandatory decoration. Each creative direction tests one project-specific mechanism in the real scene. The winner may be static.

## 1. Start from the intended response

Describe how the landing should feel when it responds and which relationship could express that: reveal, assembly, focus, transformation, depth, sequence or another project-grounded idea. Do not start with a library or fashionable effect.

## 2. Compare three intensities

For each meaningful opportunity record one `FX-*` row and compare:

- `STATIC`: a fully resolved scene without movement;
- `SIMPLE`: the smallest intervention that adds meaning;
- `EXPRESSIVE`: a stronger version when the project can support it.

Storyboards, keyframes or small prototypes are enough until a candidate proves useful. Judge meaning, fit, clarity, responsive behavior, reduced motion, feasibility and distinctiveness. Keep the weakest implementation that preserves the intended gain.

## 3. Mechanism eligibility

This section is the single operational authority for accepting or rejecting non-static behavior. Other contracts and methods may route here but must not duplicate these rules.

- `STICKY`: one visual or control must remain while two or more related content states change. Reject it for unrelated paragraphs beside a fixed image.
- `PINNED_SCROLL`: the order of states is necessary to understand a transformation or comparison. Reject it when ordinary document flow communicates equally well.
- `PARALLAX`: foreground/background relationships express real depth and the handoff supplies independent layers. Reject it for a single flat photograph.
- `HOVER`: the target is interactive or explorable and the response reveals state, preview or meaning. Reject ambient hover on non-interactive decoration.
- `VIDEO_PLAYBACK`: time, action or transformation is the content. Reject autoplay used only to make a surface feel expensive.
- `INTERACTIVE_3D`: viewpoint, manipulation or spatial form materially improves comprehension. Reject it when a render or layered 2D treatment preserves the gain.

Every selected non-static mechanism records its narrative trigger, asset decomposition, touch/mobile behavior, reduced-motion treatment and static fallback.

## 4. Test depth and 3D when relevant

Compare a depth/3D candidate when the project depends on an object, material, physical transformation, space, perspective or layers. Static imagery, 2.5D layers, rendered video and interactive 3D are alternatives. Choose 3D only if the rendered scene clearly gains from interaction or viewpoint.

Classify the delivered medium honestly:

- `FLAT_2D`: typography, image, vector or CSS composition on one visual plane;
- `LAYERED_2D`: independent real layers used for parallax, reveal or depth ordering;
- `RENDERED_3D`: frames or video produced in an external 3D authoring/rendering tool;
- `INTERACTIVE_3D`: a real model/scene rendered by an identified web runtime.

CSS/SVG gradients, shadows, skew, perspective transforms and overlapping rounded rectangles are not 3D assets. Do not use them to imitate a cheap volumetric object. Either resolve the idea as confident 2D graphic design or source/author a real external 3D scene. A selected 3D route must identify the external tool/resource, asset or runtime, rights, integration proof and static fallback.

## 5. Use references as evidence

Search by the mechanism and scene problem, not “modern effects”. Use:

- published sites for behavior in a complete experience;
- technical labs such as Codrops for isolated mechanics;
- element libraries for interaction variants;
- 3D/material sources for spatial feasibility.

Inspect the original, licensing and mobile behavior. Transfer a principle; do not copy a signature.

## 6. Integrate and prove

The selected mechanism must use real content, media and responsive layouts. It receives a static or reduced-motion fallback. Production records either:

- `FINAL` with `source/file#marker`, which the validator can locate; or
- `STATIC_WINNER_REVIEWED` with concrete visual evidence that motion weakened the scene.

One coherent defining mechanism is normally enough. Standard hover, fade-up, glow, parallax or cursor effects do not become defining merely by being present.
