# Experience Spine

The Experience Spine defines the visitor's semantic progression before art direction. It is one section inside `content-architecture.md`, not a new agent, stage, gate or project artifact.

## Ownership

02 owns the spine after comparing narrative alternatives. 01 supplies audience evidence. Later owners consume it without rewriting it: 03 translates it into art direction, 04 into composition, 05 into justified behavior and 07 reviews continuity on rendered output.

## One row per scene

For every outlined `SCN-*`, record:

- **entry state**: what the visitor currently believes, feels or lacks;
- **active question**: the question this scene must answer;
- **meaning**: the one thing that should become clear;
- **proof**: what makes that meaning credible;
- **desired shift**: the change in understanding, confidence or intent;
- **next tension or action**: why the visitor continues or acts;
- **narrative function**: `OPENING | ORIENTATION | DEMONSTRATION | CONTRAST | PROOF | DEEPENING | REST | CLIMAX | RESOLUTION | ACTION`.

The final scene may resolve into an action or explicit end. Adjacent rows must form a causal progression: the shift produced by one scene prepares the state or question of the next.

## Boundary

Do not prescribe fonts, color, imagery, layouts, sticky sections, parallax or other mechanisms here. The spine states what the experience must achieve. Visual and interaction owners decide how after direction and rendered evidence exist.

## Acceptance

G1 requires exactly one complete row for every outlined scene. Generic labels such as “inform”, “engage” or “premium” are insufficient without project-specific meaning and proof. Final build review compares the rendered landing with this spine through `EXPERIENCE_CONTINUITY`; passing requires that the intended progression remains understandable on desktop and mobile.
